# kagiboy pitch video: script draft v1

For the Colosseum Crypto World's Fair submission. Target **2:45** (the limit is 3:00), about 400 words of voiceover at a calm pace. Voice: hazy, first person. Colosseum asks for team background, the problem, who it's for, validation and vision, "a brief startup pitch, not a product demo"; the separate demo video covers the how.

Visuals reference the Drive asset folder (see `PITCH-DECK-BRIEF.md`): `photo-*` = real photos, `screen-*` = soft-LCD Game Boy screens, `site-*` = website screenshots, plus new screen recordings of kagiboy.xyz/demo. Look: white, soft pastel light, Funnel Display type, the Game Boy as the hero. Not dark.

---

### 0:00–0:14 · The flea market (hook)
**Visual:** black → a real photo fades up: two hands holding the Game Boy (`photo-home`), slow push-in. Soft chiptune boot sound. The Game Boy screen lights: `screen-01-boot` ("kagiboy").

> My first console came from a flea market. My dad bought it for me before I even knew what Nintendo was. All I knew was that I was holding something magical.

### 0:14–0:34 · The problem
**Visual:** white frame. A phone with a busy wallet signing prompt, cut quickly against notifications (illustrated, no real brands). Then a plain grey hardware wallet in a drawer, the drawer closes.

> Years later, I'm in crypto, and the most important thing I own lives on my phone. The same phone full of ads, pop-ups and links I shouldn't tap. When it asks me to sign, the screen that's asking is the same screen telling me what I'm signing. Hardware wallets fix that. But nobody loves them. They live in a drawer.

### 0:34–0:52 · The idea
**Visual:** the drawer frame dissolves into the hero render (`hero-gameboy-3q`). Title types on: **kagiboy**. Sub: "Your keys, in a Game Boy cartridge."

> So I asked: what if the safest screen I own is the one from 1989? A Game Boy has no Wi-Fi, no browser, no app store. Just a screen and a few buttons. kagiboy is a hardware wallet that's a Game Boy cartridge. You plug it into the Game Boy you already own.

### 0:52–1:22 · How it feels (three beats)
**Visual:** real photos and Game Boy screens, one per line: `photo-mash` → `screen-04-shake` → `screen-05-recovery-words` → `screen-06-choose-pin` → `screen-08-pair-code` next to the phone showing the same code → `screen-11-approve-send`, the "HOLD TO SIGN" bar fills → `screen-12-signed-confirmed`.

> Setting it up feels like starting a new game. Mash the buttons and shake the console: that's your randomness. Write down twelve words. Pick a PIN. Pair your phone with a code that shows up on both screens.
> From then on, your phone can only ask. The cartridge reads every request itself and shows the real amount and address on the Game Boy. Nothing gets signed until you hold A.

### 1:22–1:48 · It works today (validation)
**Visual:** screen recording of kagiboy.xyz/demo: the 3D Game Boy and the phone app side by side, a send approved on the Game Boy. Then the swap: quote in the app, `screen-13-approve-swap` on the Game Boy. Chain logos row in. Then `photo-boot` (the real Game Boy on a desk).

> And this isn't a mockup. The software runs today, on my own Game Boy, and in your browser at kagiboy.xyz/demo: the real Game Boy software, right next to the phone app. It signs real testnet transactions on Solana, Ethereum, Base, Arbitrum, HyperEVM and Robinhood Chain. And it gets live swap quotes from SODAX, checked and signed on the Game Boy.

### 1:48–2:08 · What's inside
**Visual:** the exploded cartridge (`site-04-stage-inside-chips` or `cartridge-exploded`), the four chips light up one by one with their labels.

> Inside the cartridge: a secure element that keeps your keys, a chip that talks to the Game Boy, and Bluetooth that only carries public data. Five wrong PINs and it wipes itself. Your twelve words bring everything back, in any standard wallet.

### 2:08–2:30 · Who it's for
**Visual:** the gallery photos as a slow grid; then the four products as clean cards (standard, limited, Japan edition, bundle with a Game Boy).

> It's for people like me, who grew up with a Game Boy and ended up holding crypto. A hundred and eighteen million Game Boys were sold. This is a wallet you'd actually keep on a shelf, or give as a gift. A cartridge for a hundred and twenty-nine dollars, numbered limited editions, and a bundle with a restored Game Boy. I live in Japan, where the old Game Boys are, and the factories are a short flight away.

### 2:30–2:47 · What's next, and the close
**Visual:** a simple three-step line: dev-board cartridge → our own board → two security audits → first 500. Then white. The Game Boy screen shows "PRESS START". End card.

> Next: a dev-board cartridge on a real Game Boy, then our own board, and two outside security audits before a single one is sold.
> Crypto should feel like something you own, not something you're afraid of.
> kagiboy. Press start.

**End card (hold 3 s):** kagiboy · **kagiboy.xyz/demo** · github.com/hazy2go/kagiboy · "Not affiliated with Nintendo."
